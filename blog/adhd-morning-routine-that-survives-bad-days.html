An ADHD morning routine that survives bad days

Many ADHD morning routines look perfect on paper and collapse by Wednesday. The problem is not willpower. It is designing a sequence that still works when sleep was short and the phone already stole twenty minutes.

What helps more than a long checklist is a short visual path: three to five steps you can see without opening five apps. Water, body movement, one priority written down, then the first work block—even if that block is only fifteen minutes.

Time blindness makes “I’ll start after I check messages” dangerous. A routine that starts with a physical cue (same mug, same playlist, same desk open) reduces the decision load.

Printable or simple digital boards beat dense productivity apps here. You want fewer choices, not more dashboards.

This week’s action: write a five-step morning card. Keep it where you make coffee. For seven days, do not add steps—only protect the sequence.
