Known, Inferred, Unknown: a simple frame for sales research

Sales research goes wrong when guesses are written as facts. “They need SEO” becomes a headline before anyone checked whether the site already ranks, publishes, or even has an indexable structure.

A simple frame fixes most of that:

  • Known — visible on a public page you opened (or stated by the buyer).
  • Inferred — a reasonable guess from Known items, still labeled as inference.
  • Unknown — missing information that should block confident claims.

This is the same discipline used in careful decision reviews: protect the buyer and your reputation by not overselling certainty.

In practice, write three short lines before any outreach draft. Example: Known — careers page lists two open roles in ops. Inferred — growth may stress current processes. Unknown — budget owner and current vendor.

Your message then references the Known line, offers help relevant to the Inferred line, and asks a question that closes an Unknown—without pretending you already know the answer.

This week’s action: take one live opportunity. Rewrite your notes into Known / Inferred / Unknown. Delete any sentence that cannot be placed in those buckets.
