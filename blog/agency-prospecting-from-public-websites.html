Agency prospecting from public websites: what is fair and useful

Public websites are the most honest first filter in B2B prospecting. They show what a company chooses to publish: services, markets, hiring, tech stack hints, blog cadence, contact options.

Fair use means staying on pages anyone can open without logging in. It means not inventing “signals” the page does not support. If the site has no case studies, that is an Unknown or a weak Evidence score—not a claim that “they urgently need marketing.”

Useful prospecting from public pages usually includes:

  • Clarity of offer and audience
  • Proof (or absence of proof) of delivery
  • Contact paths that are real (form, email, LinkedIn, phone)
  • Freshness (dates, blog, careers, product updates)

Compliant outreach starts from that evidence. Reference something visible. Ask one clear question. Do not auto-send sequences that ignore what the page actually says.

This week’s action: for five accounts, write one sentence of evidence taken only from the public homepage or about page. If you cannot find one, mark the account Unknown and deprioritize it.
